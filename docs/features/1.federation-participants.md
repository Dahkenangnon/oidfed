# 1. Federation participants

## Confirm the federation’s requirements

If you’re joining a federation or deploying software on behalf of a participant, start with the federation operator’s participation rules and your Immediate Superior’s enrollment procedure. Confirm the Entity Identifier you’ll publish, the roles you’re approved to run, and who can approve metadata and key changes. [Federation §2](https://openid.net/specs/openid-federation-1_0.html#section-2).

Ask which Trust Anchors your application should accept and how to obtain their public keys through a trusted channel. Confirm which endpoints you need to call or serve, whether they require client authentication, and how the receiver grants access. If the federation requires accreditation, ask which Trust Marks apply, who can issue them, and how to check their status.

Keep track of where the rules are published and who to contact for key rollover, suspension, or withdrawal. oidfed validates signed evidence and applies configured policy. Your organization and the federation operator decide which participants, roles, and operations are allowed.

| Confirm with the operator or Immediate Superior | Use it to configure or test |
|---|---|
| The applicable profile, approved Entity Identifier and roles, and enrollment procedure | Host and role configuration, Entity Configuration, and the Superior’s enrollment record |
| Accepted Trust Anchors and a trusted source for their public keys | `trustAnchors` and a test that rejects evidence rooted at an unrelated anchor |
| Required metadata, endpoint authentication methods, and caller grants | Published metadata, client-key lookup, and tests for denied requests |
| Required Trust Marks, accredited issuers or owners, and status-check requirements | Accreditation policy, delegation verification, and withdrawal handling |
| Rollover, evidence freshness, and emergency notification procedures | Publication, renewal, and recovery |

Choose `TrustAnchor`, `Intermediate`, or `Leaf` for the federation host, then attach the OIDC or Wallet roles the application needs. The host owns publication and federation routes; a role supplies the protocol-specific metadata and operations.

## Bring a participant into the federation

**SWAMID** enrolls **Umeå University**, which in turn enrolls its **OP**. Each participant publishes its own Entity Configuration. The Immediate Superior stores the enrollment and serves the corresponding Subordinate Statement.

**LIGO Wiki** uses **eduGAIN** as its configured Trust Anchor. It obtains eduGAIN’s identifier and keys through its trusted administrative process. It can then discover the OP and validate its chain to eduGAIN. Assign responsibility for enrollment approval, key provisioning, and deployment before testing discovery.

A useful first milestone is a consumer that accepts Umeå through the intended anchor and rejects the same evidence with an unrelated anchor set. Once that works, add protocol roles and policy. This keeps an enrollment or routing mistake separate from a later OIDC configuration failure.

## Common questions

### What is the difference between `TrustAnchor` and `Intermediate`?

A `TrustAnchor` represents a root of trust for its participants and can also specify `authorityHints` to participate under another federation. Its Trust Mark issuer/owner policy remains available in this configuration. An `Intermediate` is a subordinate authority that must list at least one superior authority in `authorityHints`. Intermediates also cannot carry `trustMarkIssuers` or `trustMarkOwners` metadata block configurations since those claims are reserved for Trust Anchors.

### How do I enroll a subordinate entity?

Use `await ta.registerSubordinate(record)` to validate publication claims, sanitize metadata, supply timestamps, and record configured lifecycle events in the same transaction. Direct `storage.subordinate.register(record)` calls bypass that lifecycle layer. Adapter implementations and applications using low-level writes must supply complete records and maintain the required validation and event updates themselves; see [storage adapters](11.6.storage-adapters.md).

### Why does subordinate storage reject my metadata?

A subordinate's metadata block in the storage record must not carry operational fields (such as `federation_fetch_endpoint` or `federation_list_endpoint`) inside `metadata.federation_entity`. Import `sanitizeSubordinateMetadata(metadata)` from `@oidfed/core` to strip those fields before calling `registerSubordinate()` or `updateSubordinate()`.

Subordinate metadata excludes operational endpoint fields. Federation §§8.1, 8.2, and 8.4 require Entity Configuration publication for fetch, list, and Trust Mark Status endpoints; Extended Listing §4 imports the list restriction. oidfed also reserves the other operational endpoint fields and authentication companions to the host's Entity Configuration as a library policy.

### Is explicit client registration handled automatically?

Compose an `OidcProviderRole` from `@oidfed/oidc` into the entity's `roles`. The parent initializes its registration handler and dispatches matching requests through `handleRequest()`. Connect registration hooks to your OP's client store. Automatic registration still needs a call from the OP's authorization or PAR flow.
