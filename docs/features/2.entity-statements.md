# 2. Entity Statements

**Umeå University OP** publishes its own **Entity Configuration**. **eduGAIN** issues a **Subordinate Statement** about **Umeå University OP**. Both are signed Entity Statements, but they make different assertions. [Federation §3](https://openid.net/specs/openid-federation-1_0.html#section-3).

Use the host's `getEntityConfiguration()` for its own publication. A Superior uses `registerSubordinate()` and `getSubordinateStatement()` for approved subjects. The configured keyring and host options supply signing and lifetime; normal applications do not assemble these JWTs by hand.

## Follow the two statements

Umeå's Entity Configuration says what Umeå publishes about itself. eduGAIN's Subordinate Statement says what the authority attests about Umeå. Both are signed, but their issuers, subjects, permitted claims, and signing keys differ. A federation chain needs the appropriate relationship evidence; a collection of self-signed configurations is not that chain.

Build the payload with the matching API, sign it with the issuer's federation key, and serve it through the appropriate endpoint. For inspection, decode the result and compare the identity and time claims with the intended publication. For acceptance, validate the evidence through the chain workflow.

When a participant changes keys or metadata, the next issued statements can change while older copies remain usable within their signed lifetimes. Plan refresh and rollover together with publication, rather than treating the JWT as a permanent configuration file.

## Common questions

### Why does the Leaf constructor throw when my metadata block contains endpoints?

Leaf entities are client or provider endpoints at the edge of the trust tree and do not issue subordinate statements or list subordinates. Therefore, they are forbidden from publishing `federation_fetch_endpoint` or `federation_list_endpoint` in their `federation_entity` metadata block.
